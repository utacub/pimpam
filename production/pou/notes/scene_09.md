# Scene 10 · `scene_09.glsl` · Citipati brooding at Ukhaa Tolgod

## What happens
A *Citipati osmolskae* sits on her nest on the rippled orange-red sand of Ukhaa Tolgod. It is early morning: the sun is low on the
left and soft in the haze, the sky is pale with a warm horizon, and buttes and cliffs of red Djadochta sandstone stand in the middle
distance, paler with the distance. Long shadows fall to the right: hers, and those of a few clumps of low shrubs.
The nest is a tight ring of sixteen elongated blue-green eggs laid in pairs. They slope inwards (the upper ends towards the middle) and
are half buried in a low mound of sand around an empty centre. She sits in that open middle with her feathered arms spread back over
the ring, as in the brooding specimen IGM 100/979. The long flight feathers drape over the eggs.
Scale: .45 screen units per metre (about 2.9 m long).

- 0–3.7 s: brooding, still (breathing, slow head turns, blinks).
- 3.75–4.9 s, **first beat**: she lowers her head and looks down at the eggs (something is tapping inside one).
- 4.0–4.55 s: the wings fold in to her sides. 4.0–4.4 the weight goes forward.
- 4.25–5.2 s, **she rises** on her long legs and stands in the middle of the ring: all the eggs show. The tail fan spreads a little
  (4.3–5.8). 4.6–5.4 her head goes down towards the eggs, and from then on it follows the egg that is hatching.
- **Hatching (key moment)**, three eggs, each the same way: the shell jiggles in little bursts (tapping from inside) while a crack
  line grows round it; the top comes off and tips over onto the rim of the mound beside the egg, where it stays (the open bottom stays
  in the sand); a wet, dark chick pushes out of the opening, climbs out and sits in front of its shell; over the next 1.5 s it dries
  into a pale fluffball.

## Evidence followed
- *Citipati osmolskae* (Clark, Norell & Barsbold 2001), Djadochta Formation at Ukhaa Tolgod, Mongolia (Campanian, about 75 Ma),
  aeolian sands. Short deep skull with a toothless beak and a tall crest over the snout; long neck; long legs; short tail ending in a
  pygostyle (a fan of tail feathers is likely, as in other oviraptorosaurs: Persons et al. 2014).
- Brooding posture: the adult sits in the open centre of a ring of eggs, legs folded, arms spread over the clutch, as in the "Big Mama"
  specimen IGM 100/979 (Norell et al. 1995; Clark et al. 1999): the arms spread back round the clutch, so the arm feathers would
  have covered the eggs. Oviraptorid clutches are tight rings of elongated eggs laid in pairs, sloping inwards, half buried in a low
  mound of sand around an open centre (Norell et al. 1995; Varricchio et al. 1997).
- Wing: pennaceous feathers on the forearm and hand (long remiges), with rows of coverts over their roots, as in *Caudipteryx* and
  other oviraptorosaurs with preserved feathers. The upper arm stays hidden in the body plumage.
- Setting: the Djadochta red beds (aeolian sandstones, dunes and interdunes) of Ukhaa Tolgod and Bayanzag. The buttes and cliffs are
  today's eroded red beds, used as a backdrop.
- Egg colour: blue-green, from biliverdin and protoporphyrin traces in oviraptorid eggshell (Wiemann et al. 2017, 2018).

## Unverified / artistic choices
- All colours (grey-brown plumage with buff-fringed coverts, orange-red crest, blue-grey bare skin round the eye) are invented.
- The clutch is simplified to one ring of eight pairs (real clutches often have two or three stacked rings, the lower ones fully
  buried). The eggs are drawn a little larger than life so that they read at this size.
- The buttes in the background are the modern landscape: in the Campanian the area was a dune field. They stand for the red beds.
- Hatching: three chicks hatch together in a few seconds (much faster than in life) and the chicks' downy look is speculative.
  Oviraptorid embryos are known (Norell et al. 1994), but not their plumage. Whether a parent watched the hatching is not known.
- The coo (closed-mouth vocalisation) is inferred from birds and crocodiles; it is a guess.
- Rising to stand over the eggs and then sitting again is our staging.

## Changes after the review (frames 4.7, 7.0, 10.3, 12.5 in /tmp/pou_review/scene_09/)
1. Internal outlines removed. The thigh, the folded wing and the arm have no contour inside the body. A contour appears only where
   they stand out of the body's outline. The thigh is modelled by light (lit on the sun's side, darker below) and casts a soft shadow
   on the body. The white wing bar is gone.
2. The wing is a feathered wing in one piece: 11 remiges (5 on the forearm, 6 on the hand), a row of greater coverts, a row of median
   coverts and the small marginal coverts. Each feather lies over the next with a lighter free edge and a soft shadow on the one below,
   and the feathers vary slightly in value. There are no dark bars.
   - When brooding, the arm is spread back along her side and the long feathers drape down and back over the eggs onto the sand.
   - When standing, the wing folds bird-like (the wrist forward, the hand back).
   - The arm bones keep their lengths through the fold.
3. The nest is a tight ring of 8 pairs of elongated eggs, sloping inwards and half buried in a low mound of sand around an empty
   centre. The hatched bottoms stay in the sand, and their tops lie tipped over on the rim. Each egg has a small shadow on the sand.
4. The neck has one tube only, and it takes its countershading from its own midline (nape darker, throat paler) in any pose. The
   feather texture follows the neck, so there is no seam and no pale band where the head goes down.
5. Backdrop and light:
   - a hazy pale sky with a warm horizon and a low sun;
   - red-orange sandstone buttes and cliffs (in two layers, with aerial perspective) and pale far mesas;
   - a low dune, and ripples with a lit side and a shaded lee side;
   - sparse clumps of low shrubs with long shadows;
   - her long shadow on the sand. It is also cast on the front eggs and follows her pose: sitting, standing, the legs, the neck.
   - The cumulus clouds and their shadows are removed.
