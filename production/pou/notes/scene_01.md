# Scene 2 (scene_01.glsl) · Smilodon fatalis at La Brea

## What happens

It is late afternoon on the grassland of La Brea (today's Los Angeles) in the Late Pleistocene. The low sun is behind the animals on the
left.

- **The setting:**
  - Far ranges (San Gabriel-like) are layered in aerial perspective: pale lilac far away, blue-grey nearer, olive foothills, with spurs
    and gullies on the sunward slopes.
  - Groves of oak and juniper stand in the middle distance.
  - Golden bunchgrass grows in clumps of varied height and density, finer towards the horizon and taller at the sides of the
    foreground. It is grazed short round the animals. The seed heads glow against the light and slow waves of wind run through them.
  - On the left lies a calm asphalt seep: a black glossy pool that mirrors the sky and the far range, with a tar-stained muddy rim, a
    few leaves and twigs on it, and cattails only at its edge.
  - Pale butterflies drift by and pollen specks float in the light.
- **The mother:** a Smilodon lies sphinx-like on a low rise, facing left. She is robust, with massive forelimbs and wrists, a thick
  neck, a deep chest, high withers and a back sloping to lower hips. She has a short bobtail and a long muzzle with small rounded ears,
  and her sabres show below the lip line. Her forepaws lie flat in front with the toes showing; her hind leg is folded under with the
  hock on the ground.
  - She is lit from behind: a warm rim on her back, head and ears; her near side is in soft shadow with the sky's blue fill. Her back is
    darker and her belly and throat paler.
  - Her shadow falls on the grass towards the viewer.
- **The cubs:** two kittens lie by her forepaws. They have big paws, round heads with short muzzles, small ears and a faint spotted coat.

The story:

- **From 3.8:** the cubs play, always on four legs.
  - The far cub (B) crouches, wiggles and pounces towards its sibling.
  - The near cub (A) rolls onto its back and bats and kicks up with its paws while B swipes at it with a forepaw. Both mew.
  - B lies down again (7.2–7.9). A rolls back onto its belly, crouches and bounds off towards its mother's tail.
- **4.2–6.6, the key moment:** the mother gives a slow, sleepy yawn.
  - Her head rises and tilts back, her ears go back and her eyes squint shut.
  - The jaw opens wide and slowly, showing both sabres, the tongue and the small teeth. It holds, then closes.
  - She licks her nose, then stays drowsy with half-closed eyes.
- **8.6–9.4:** cub A pounces on her bobtail and pins it with its forepaws, and her ear twitches. The tail flicks up out of its paws with
  a damped swing, and the kitten rolls onto its back after it, batting at it with its paws up. Then it rolls back and lies watching.
- **9.6–11.2, the echo:** cub B lifts its head towards its mother. She lifts her head and gives a soft chuff: a short nod with flared
  nostrils and her whiskers twitching forward. Then she lowers her head and licks the top of B's head twice. The cub shuts its eyes
  under the tongue.
- **After 11.4:** B gives a small mew. The mother's head rises back to rest, and both cubs lay their heads down (12.4–13.9). It is calm.

## Evidence followed, and what is unverified

- **Smilodon fatalis anatomy.**
  - It was very robust, with massive forelimbs, a thick neck and a short tail; the skeletons from Rancho La Brea show these.
  - The upper canines are about 18 cm long in the crown, and the lower jaw has a flange. Here the sabres are about 0.18 m and sit below
    the lips, with the tips by the chin.
  - How much the lips covered the sabres is **debated**.
  - The shape of the head and the ears in life is **inferred** from living cats.
- **Coat.** The coat colour and pattern are **unknown**. As commissioned, I chose a tawny coat, darker along the back and paler
  underneath (countershading), with very faint, low-contrast spots on the flanks. This is inference from living cats.
- **Cubs.**
  - They have milk teeth only: the permanent sabres erupted late and finished growing at about 3 years (from the growth studies of La
    Brea juveniles).
  - The faint spotted cub coat is **speculative**, by analogy with lion and puma cubs.
- **Behaviour.**
  - Maternal care and social life in Smilodon are inferred from healed injuries and juvenile remains at La Brea, and are **debated**.
  - The yawn, the nose lick, play (pouncing, rolling on the back, batting at a tail) and the licking of a cub are generic felid
    behaviour.
  - The chuff, a friendly greeting puff, is a **behavioural hypothesis**: among living big cats it is known in tigers, snow leopards and
    clouded leopards.
- **The yawn gape.** It is shown at about 65° plus the lift of the head, so it is conservative. Smilodon's maximum gape is estimated at
  about 110°, but a yawn would not need it.
- **Setting.**
  - The asphalt seeps of Rancho La Brea are real. The ranges are a generic San Gabriel-like backdrop, not a survey of the real skyline.
  - The vegetation is simplified to bunchgrass with groves of oak and juniper; the real mosaic also had sage scrub and chaparral.
  - The cattails at the seep and the sulphur-like butterflies are generic.

## Frame time

Measured with `tools/pou-frames.mjs` at 960×540, pinned to one core with `taskset`, interleaved with the reference scene 1.

- **Scene 1 (the reference):** 4.0–4.5 s single-core, which is the brief's "about 1.0 s".
- **This scene (3.6, 5.2, 6.2, 9.2, 10.8, 11.0):** 4.6–5.5 s single-core, so about 1.15–1.35× the reference, roughly 1.2–1.3 s on
  the brief's scale. The heaviest moment is the lick (11.0), where the head is drawn a second time over the cub.

**Finding about this environment.** SwiftShader here runs the code behind every `if`, so bounding-box early returns do not lower the
time; even a loop that runs zero times executes its body once, masked. The cost follows the amount of code.

So the scene keeps its bounding boxes (they help on a real GPU), but saves time in these ways:

- The grass clumps are drawn in loops over only the rows that touch the pixel, one piece per row.
- Parts that never overlap share one piece: the mother's whole body; the far ear and far leg; the cub's tail, far legs and far ear;
  the cub's near legs; the teeth; the cavity and tongue.
- The modelling of the forms comes from cheap ellipsoid normals, not from extra pieces.
- Thin lines (lips, the closed eye, the nostril, the cub's mouth) use shadow or veil.
- Only the nearest butterfly is drawn.
