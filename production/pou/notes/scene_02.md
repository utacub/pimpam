# Scene 3 (scene_02.glsl) · The woolly rhinoceros and the giant deer on the glacial steppe

## What happens

A winter morning on the glacial steppe of Eurasia in the Late Pleistocene. The low sun stands on the left, a little behind the animals.
The sky is pale turquoise.

- **The setting:**
  - A high far range lies under snow and glaciers, pale in the haze. In front of it is a lower range, bluer, with dark rock on its steep
    faces.
  - On the low hills of the middle distance stand woods of dark spruce and pine with a few bare larches, frosted. They rise into a low
    ridge behind the stag.
  - The plain is snow with wind ripples, and it is lit warm on the sunward faces and blue in the shade. Tussocks of dry, straw-coloured
    steppe grass stick through the snow, with now and then a red-brown dwarf willow. The tussocks are finer far away and taller in the
    foreground.
  - Diamond dust glints in the air near the ground.
- **The rhino** (in front, facing left): a woolly rhinoceros about 2 m at the shoulder.
  - It has a high hump of fat and hair over the shoulders, a back sloping down to a rounded rump, and a barrel body whose belly line sags a
    little between the legs. Its legs are short, thick and three-toed, and its short tail ends in a tuft.
  - The long, large head (about a quarter of the body length) is carried low, with the muzzle near the ground. The long front horn is
    flattened from side to side, so we see it as a broad blade curving forward and up, with a pale worn front edge. The second horn on
    the forehead is small.
  - The coat is warm red-brown, in long locks that flow down and back from the hump and the neck. The locks are longest on the throat,
    the hump and the belly fringe, and shorter on the face and the legs. They vary in length, tilt and value; the hump is bleached paler
    and the belly and legs are darker. It has hoar frost on its back, small eyes and ears with tufts.
  - The low sun catches the lock tips along the hump and the face with a warm rim. Its long blue shadow runs to the right across the
    snow.
- **The stag** (further back on the right, its body facing left and its head turned to us): a Megaloceros stag about 2.1 m at the
  shoulder.
  - It has broad palmate antlers with tines along their upper edges and snow lying on the palms.
  - It is robust, with a thick neck and a heavy chest and legs. Its coat is fawn-brown, with a dark hump over the withers, a dark band down the shoulder, a dark collar and throat mane, and a pale
    belly and rump.

The story:

- **Until 4.0:** calm. Both animals breathe vapour now and then. They blink and flick their ears, the rhino's tail swings, and the grass
  sways.
- **4.0–7.0:** the rhino lowers its head to the snow and sweeps it from side to side with its front horn, five strokes about 0.62 s apart.
  - At each stroke the horn turns towards us or away from us, and a burst of snow clumps and powder flies up round the horn.
  - Beside its muzzle, a patch of steppe grass is uncovered and grows, with the snow heaped round its edges.
- **5.45–6.6, the key moment:** the stag shakes its head, a damped shake from side to side about 3 times a second.
  - The snow on its palms breaks up and falls in a cascade of clumps (lit from behind, blue in their shade) and a veil of powder, past
    its head and shoulders to the snow. It settles by about 7.5.
- **7.0–8.05:** the rhino steps forward onto the patch, one foot at a time (near fore, near hind, far fore, far hind), and grazes. It
  tears at the grass three times (about 7.9, 8.7 and 9.5) and chews.
- **7.6–8.15:** the stag shifts one forefoot.
- **9.35–10.95, the echo:** the stag lifts its muzzle and gives a soft bellow (mouth open 9.6–10.45). A big cloud of breath rises in
  front of its face, then a second, smaller one.
- **10.0–10.6:** the rhino lifts its head, chewing, with a wisp of grass in its mouth, and snorts: a puff of breath round its muzzle
  (about 10.35, and a smaller one at 10.5).
- **12.1–12.9:** it lowers its head back to rest. Calm afterwards: breaths, blinks, ears, the tail, the grass.

## Evidence followed, and what is unverified

- **Woolly rhinoceros (Coelodonta antiquitatis).**
  - The hump over the shoulders (tall spines carrying a heavy, low head), the long skull carried low, and the broad lip of a grazer
    follow the skeletons and the frozen carcasses from Siberia.
  - The front horn is long and flattened from side to side.
  - Front horns found in permafrost show a worn, flattened front surface, and this is often explained as wear from sweeping snow aside
    to reach the grass. I know this explanation only from **secondary sources**; it is an interpretation, not a direct observation.
  - The coat colour (reddish-brown, paler tips) follows the hair of frozen carcasses. Hair colour can change after death, so it is
    **unverified**.
- **Megaloceros giganteus.**
  - The brief asked for "antlers over 4 m across". The largest known antler spans are about 3.6 m, so the antlers here span about 3.5 m.
  - The hump over the withers, the dark band down the shoulder and the dark collar follow cave art (Cougnac, Chauvet). The colours of the
    rest of the coat are **inferred** from living deer.
  - Antlers on a stag in winter: deer grow their antlers in summer and shed them in late winter or spring, so carrying them in early
    winter is plausible.
  - Its voice is **unknown**. The soft bellow is inferred from living deer and kept gentle, as the content rules require.
- **Coexistence.** Both species lived on the steppes of Eurasia in the Late Pleistocene.
- **Setting.**
  - The vegetation is simplified to dry steppe grasses, dwarf willow and woods of spruce, pine and larch in the distance. The real
    mammoth-steppe mosaic was richer.
  - The thin snow cover with grass showing through suits the dry, cold steppe, but it is not a reconstruction of a particular place.
